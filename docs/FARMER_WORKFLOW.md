# Field Shift farmer walkthrough

## Find a location

1. Open Explore the basin. The site starts on the full Murray–Darling Basin. Choose Northern or Southern Basin, choose a town weather sample, or use a point on the map. More area filters includes state, catchment and local government areas. The pin and drawing tools delineate an area. The site identifies where an actual measured series is available.
2. Drag the map or use the arrow keys while the map is focused. Use + and − for zoom. After clicking the map, the mouse wheel zooms around the pointer. Full basin extent returns to the basin overview.
3. Choose Basin boundaries for the bundled offline map, Street map for road and place context, or Satellite for real Esri satellite and aerial imagery. Street and Satellite require an internet connection. Earth observation measurements and 3D moisture values use a separate data layer. The background image alone supplies no numeric farm data.

## Read evidence and choose a rotation

4. Under Check your growing conditions, review historical weather, rainfall and drought, heat, surface water, moisture, and vegetation. History defaults to 2016–2025. Expand History to adjust the date range. Check the observation date and geographic footprint before using a number. Real mode shows the available records. Example mode demonstrates the separate satellite workflows with synthetic values.
5. Open Compare rotations. Under Farm inputs and priorities, set the current rotation, enter known soil information and weight water, soil, ground cover and crop fit. Compare the baseline against alternatives. Each total uses common evidence. If a weighted input is missing, the page names it. Compare with available evidence explicitly omits the same unsupported priorities for every rotation, keeps the saved priorities, and labels the result as partial. Use this plan chooses the rotation for the calendar and brief.
6. Open Future estimates to adjust warming, rainfall and heavy storms. This is a sensitivity test against historical observations. Compare the change and reset when needed.
7. Open Calendar from the header. Browse months and view the activity scheduled for the selected month. Expand all 36 months for the full rotation. Use Farm brief to review evidence and checks, and print or save the plan as a PDF.

## Data and validation still needed

| Need | Where it matters |
| --- | --- |
| GPM IMERG Final and provisional Late rainfall, with date windows and coverage | Dedicated drought history, recent rainfall and heavy-rain analysis. Current POWER precipitation supplies a separately labeled proxy. |
| SMAP Level 4 surface and root-zone values and grid quality | Real moisture trends, 3D moisture layer and the draft October moisture check for sorghum. |
| OPERA surface-water classifications, valid masks and comparable pre-event scenes | Real possible-inundation summaries. Document permanent water, comparable coverage and area processing. |
| Dated HLS scenes and cloud/coverage QA | Real linked before/after vegetation viewer. Street and Satellite map styles supply geographic context. |
| MODIS NDVI with QA | Historical ground-cover evidence used in rotation explanations. |
| Depth-specific SLGA CaCl₂ pH, clay and site soil details | Soil suitability and crop matching. The runtime currently uses user-entered pH where supplied. |
| Valid ECOSTRESS ET and stress or a documented MODIS ET/PET alternative | Water-use and evaporative-demand context. The previous urban-point trial yielded no usable ET. |
| Regional processing and coverage inventory | Detailed numerical and satellite coverage across both basin regions before release. Point weather is available for six towns. Other selections need appropriately supported series or aggregates. |
| Local crop parameters and independent validation | Validate crop-specific soil limits, water requirements and degree-days, the sorghum moisture gate, local weather and flood comparisons, and grower-entered market or forage information. |
| Publication and usability checks | Review basemap attribution/terms for public deployment, farmer usability, mobile touch behavior and print pagination under real workloads. |
